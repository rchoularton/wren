# Reviewer-Response Prose Rules

When drafting or editing a point-by-point response to journal reviewers (any
reviewer-response document under `papers/*/reviews/`), follow this standard.

1. **Verbatim reviewer quote** for every comment — never paraphrase or summarise it.
2. **Five-part structure per comment:** quote → formal, deferential acknowledgement →
   a one-or-two-sentence overall response → detailed response in academic prose → a
   concise bulleted "Changes made" change-log (the only place shorthand is allowed).
3. **Complete academic prose, not shorthand**, in the response body. Explain *why*,
   not just *that* a change was made; be specific and quantitative.
4. **Voice:** first-person plural; open formal and deferential, then gracious-but-
   confident; defend declines with evidence; never dismiss a comment.
5. **Proportionate depth:** full structure for Major/Moderate comments; brief but still
   formal prose for Minor corrections.
6. **Must not read as AI-written.** Avoid em-dashes, formulaic connectives ("Moreover",
   "Furthermore", "It is important to note"), uniform sentence rhythm, and hedging
   filler. Vary sentence length; write the way a careful human researcher would. This
   applies to every drafted manuscript edit too, not just the response document.
